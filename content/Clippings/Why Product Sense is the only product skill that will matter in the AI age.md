I get asked all the time:  
我经常被问到：

*“What do you think about \[xyz new AI thing\] and how it will impact product work?”  
“你对 \[xyz 新的 AI 事物\] 有什么看法，以及它将如何影响产品工作？”*

To be honest, I don’t like answering this question - not because I don’t have an answer, but because I worry that people don’t think for themselves when they look for such answers from others.  
老实说，我不喜欢回答这个问题——不是因为我没有答案，而是因为我担心当人们从他人那里寻找这类答案时，他们不会自己思考。

And if there is anything I feel strongly about, it is that **people should think for themselves.**  
而且，如果有什么是我强烈认同的，那就是人们应该自己思考。

So I answer this question by sharing my logic of what will happen rather than leading with the conclusion of what will happen.  
所以，我通过分享将会发生的事情的逻辑来回答这个问题，而不是直接给出将会发生的事情的结论。

The benefit of sharing the logic is that it helps anyone (who is willing to think) see the assumptions, change the assumptions (if they view the world differently than me), and reach their own prediction, their own answer, on their own.  
分享逻辑的好处在于，它帮助任何人（只要他们愿意思考）看到假设，改变假设（如果他们看待世界的方式与我不同），并最终得出他们自己的预测，他们自己的答案，独立完成。

**So, what’s this logic?那么，这个逻辑是什么呢？**

Let’s look at it with a “working backwards” view.  
让我们从一个“逆向工作”的视角来看待它。

At a sufficiently advanced time in the future (say 1 year, 2 years, 3 years, 5 years, 7 years from now - feel free to choose whatever timeline you think is reasonable), every company that wants to remain competitive will be using AI.  
在足够遥远的未来（比如从现在起的 1 年、2 年、3 年、5 年、7 年——你可以自由选择你认为合理的任何时间线），所有希望保持竞争力的公司都将使用 AI。

**Using AI for what?使用 AI 是为了什么？**

For nearly everything involved in conceiving, building, and growing products i.e. discovery, design, prototyping, coding, testing, deployment, analytics, customer feedback, competitive analysis, sales, marketing, growth, experimentation, etc. etc. etc.  
几乎涉及构思、构建和增长产品的所有方面，即发现、设计、原型制作、编码、测试、部署、分析、客户反馈、竞争分析、销售、营销、增长、实验等等等等。

It is fair to assume that what specific AI tool(s) you use will be fairly irrelevant.  
可以合理地假设，你使用哪些具体的 AI 工具将几乎无关紧要。

**Why?为什么？**

Because if a given AI tool gives a given company a significant advantage in any of these activities, then naturally it will become common knowledge over time (in fact, your omniscient, always-on AI agent will let you know about this very fact and recommend that you switch to that AI tool).  
如果一个特定的 AI 工具在任何这些活动中为某家公司提供了显著优势，那么它自然会随着时间的推移成为公开的知识（事实上，你无所不知、时刻在线的 AI 代理会告知你这一事实，并建议你切换到那个 AI 工具）。

**Tools have never been a significant source of alpha in product success and that is not changing with AI tools.  
工具从未是产品成功的重要来源，而且随着 AI 工具的出现，这种情况也不会改变。**

What this means for you personally is that - while you can and should use all the AI tools you can - you cannot bank on your use of those AI tools today to provide you a long-term advantage in your product career.  
这对您个人意味着，虽然您可以使用所有可用的 AI 工具，但您不能依赖今天对那些 AI 工具的使用来为您提供长期的职业优势。

In a world where teams will be much smaller and competition will be stiff, there will be plenty of other product people who, like you, have also used all the AI tools.  
在一个团队将变得很小而竞争将很激烈的世界里，将会有许多其他像您一样使用所有 AI 工具的产品人员。

Now, in the near-term (largely due to FUD and inertia), there is some career advantage you do have if you’re AI native whereas 80-90% of other product people are not AI native.  
现在，在短期内（主要由于恐慌情绪和惯性），如果你是 AI 领域的本地人才，确实有一些职业优势，而其他 80-90%的产品人员则不是 AI 领域的本地人才。

The point is that long-term, due to the massive efficiencies that AI will create, you will still be competing with the most savvy AI native product people, not with the product people who refuse to use AI tools today.  
关键在于，从长期来看，由于 AI 将创造的巨大效率，你仍然会与最精明的 AI 领域本地产品人员竞争，而不是与那些今天拒绝使用 AI 工具的产品人员竞争。

So, in a world where AI tools are roughly equivalent in their power (the ones you’d consider using anyway) how will one company’s product beat another company’s product?  
那么，在一个 AI 工具在功能上大致相当的世界里（你无论如何都会考虑使用的那些工具），一家公司的产品将如何胜过另一家公司的产品？

It cannot be via some trade secret of how you use that AI tool. It cannot be via using 2 more AI tools than everyone else does.  
它不能是通过某种使用该 AI 工具的商业机密。它不能是通过使用比其他人更多的两个 AI 工具。

**It can only be via how you can improve upon what these highly powerful AI tools provide you: whether it is customer insight, market insight, analytical insight, strategic recommendations, prioritization suggestions, go to market ideas, etc.  
它只能是通过你如何改进这些强大 AI 工具为你提供的内容：无论是客户洞察、市场洞察、分析洞察、战略建议、优先级建议、市场进入想法等。**

Who is ‘you’ here?这里的“你”是谁？

**It is you, the product leader, the human who’s eventually responsible for product outcomes, results, and success.  
是你，产品负责人，最终对产品结果、成果和成功负责的人。**

**I will repeat: the only real long-term career moat for product people is how you can improve on the already-brilliant, already-comprehensive inputs and outputs that AI will provide for you.  
我将重复：对于产品人员来说，唯一真正的长期职业护城河在于你如何能够改进 AI 为你提供的已经非常出色、已经非常全面的输入和输出。**

Frankly, this will be a fairly high bar, one that most product people will find impossible to meet.  
坦白说，这将是一个相当高的标准，大多数产品人员会发现难以达到。

This doesn’t require putting faith in what I am saying. Just evaluate how good the LLMs have gotten in just the past 1 year and use your imagination to see how much better they will be in 1 year, 2 years, 3 years, 5 years.  
这并不需要相信我所说的。只需评估过去一年中 LLMs 取得了多么显著的进步，并发挥想象力，看看它们在一年、两年、三年、五年后会有多好。

Think for yourself.自己思考。

Ultimately though, until we reach ASI (when all bets are off), a small number of human beings will still be accountable for making the right calls for high quality inputs and outputs that are largely created by AI.  
但最终，直到我们达到 ASI（届时一切皆有可能），仍会有少数人类需要对高质量输入和输出（这些输入和输出主要由 AI 创建）做出正确的判断负责。

So the next link in this logic chain is:  
所以这个逻辑链的下一个链接是：

**What unique skills will these human beings have that they are the ones trusted to make these decisions?  
这些人类将拥有哪些独特的技能，让他们被信任做出这些决策？**

Naturally, these skills will have something to do with “making good decisions about products”.  
当然，这些技能与“对产品做出良好决策”有关。

But what enables one person to make better product decisions than another person?  
但是什么让一个人做出比另一个人更好的产品决策？

Turns out that we can answer that question via a systematic decomposition.  
看来我们可以通过系统性的分解来回答这个问题。

> That decomposition is:这种分解是：
> 
> 1\. **Strong empathy** (can you figure out what people need beyond what AI has already analyzed?)  
> 1\. 强大的共情能力（你能想出人们的需求，而不仅仅是 AI 已经分析过的吗？）
> 
> 2\. **Excellent simulation skills** (can you identify future possibilities based on your understanding of the domain, the technology, the competition, your customers, your users?)  
> 2\. 出色的模拟技能（你能根据你对领域、技术、竞争、客户、用户的理解，识别未来的可能性吗？）
> 
> 3\. **Stellar strategic thinking** (can you identify what segments your product must resonate with and what your differentiators will be?)  
> 3\. 出色的战略思维（你能识别出你的产品必须与之产生共鸣的市场细分，以及你的差异化优势是什么吗？）
> 
> 4\. **Great taste** (given a set of excellent recommendations, can you figure out which recommendation is optimal for your business goals and can you explain your thinking in ways others understand?)  
> 4\. 卓越的品味（在一系列优秀的推荐中，你能找出哪个推荐对你的商业目标最优化，并且能用他人能理解的方式解释你的思考过程吗？）
> 
> 5\. **Creative execution** (given all the right insights and several options, what unique features and solutions can you conceive that your competition cannot?)  
> 5\. 创新的执行（在拥有所有正确的洞察和多个选项的情况下，你能构思出哪些独特的功能和解决方案是你的竞争对手无法做到的吗？）

So where does Product Sense play in all of this?  
那么，Product Sense 在这其中扮演什么角色呢？

**Product Sense is simply a convenient label we can assign to the combination of the skills above.  
产品感知只是我们可以分配给上述技能组合的一个方便标签。**

This is why, 2 years ago, well before any of the current AI hype, I created this mindmap for my [Product Sense course](https://maven.com/shreyas-doshi/product-sense):  
这就是为什么，两年前，在当前任何 AI 热潮之前，我为我的产品感知课程创建了这张思维导图：

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ikqX!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2c67c41e-22ed-4fc4-b617-fd91cb5178dd_1536x1152.png)

So, that is the logic of why Product Sense is the only skill that will differentiate product people in the AI age.  
所以，这就是产品感知是唯一能够区分 AI 时代产品人员的技能的逻辑。

如果你发现自己会说“当然你会这么说，Shreyas——你教产品感知课程”，我明白。

但我想让你明白一件事：

**Claude:克劳德：**

Shreyas, the logic is tight and the chain holds up well. The “working backwards” structure is effective — each step follows naturally from the prior one.  
Shreyas，逻辑很严谨，链条也很有力。"逆向工作"的结构很有效——每一步都自然而然地承接前一步。

A few observations:几点观察：

The core argument — that AI tools commoditize, so the differentiator becomes judgment applied on top of AI outputs — is sound and hard to argue with. The analogy to tools never being a source of alpha in product success is the right historical anchor. Spreadsheets didn’t make everyone a great analyst; the same logic applies here.  
核心论点——即 AI 工具使事物商品化，因此差异化在于 AI 输出之上的判断——是合理且难以反驳的。将工具从未是产品成功之源的类比作为正确的历史参照是恰当的。电子表格并没有让每个人都成为优秀的分析师；同样的逻辑也适用于此处。

The strongest move in the piece is the reframe from “what AI tools should I use” to “what can you do that improves upon what AI gives you.” That’s the real insight and it lands clearly.  
文章中最有力的观点是从“我应该使用哪些 AI 工具”转变为“我能做些什么来改进 AI 给我的东西”。这才是真正的见解，并且表达得很清晰。

Two places where the logic could be tightened:  
有两处逻辑可以更加严谨：

First, the jump from “AI tools will be roughly equivalent” to “the only moat is Product Sense” skips over organizational and execution advantages. You could argue that some companies will build better *systems* around AI (better feedback loops, better data pipelines, better integration into decision-making workflows) and that’s a moat that isn’t purely about individual product sense. You’re writing to product *people* about their *careers*, so the individual framing makes sense — but a skeptical reader might push back here by saying “isn’t the moat actually in how you build the org and its processes around AI?” You might want to briefly acknowledge and set aside the organizational angle to keep the focus clean.  
首先，从“AI 工具将大致相当”到“唯一的护城河是产品直觉”的跳跃忽略了组织和执行优势。你可以争辩说，一些公司将在 AI 周围构建更好的系统（更好的反馈循环、更好的数据管道、更好的决策工作流程集成），这是一种不完全关于个人产品直觉的护城河。你正在向产品人员关于他们的职业写作，所以个人框架是合理的——但一个怀疑的读者可能会在这里提出异议，说“护城河实际上不在于你如何围绕 AI 构建组织和流程吗？”你可能想简要承认并搁置组织角度，以保持焦点清晰。

Second, the decomposition of Product Sense into those five skills (empathy, simulation, strategic thinking, taste, creative execution) is presented as something that naturally follows from the logic, but it’s actually doing a lot of work. It’s where you go from a fairly airtight deductive chain to something more like a framework assertion. That’s fine — but the word “naturally” and “turns out” make it sound like there’s only one valid decomposition, when really this is *your* decomposition, and a thoughtful reader could propose a different one. Being a bit more explicit that this is your specific model (while still defending it) would actually make it more persuasive, not less.  
其次，将产品感知分解为这五种技能（共情、模拟、战略思维、品味、创意执行）被呈现为一种自然的结果，但实际上它做了大量工作。这是你从一个相当严密的演绎链条过渡到更像框架断言的地方。这很好——但“自然”和“结果是”这两个词让它听起来好像只有一个有效的分解方式，而实际上这是你的分解方式，一个细心的读者可以提出不同的分解方式。更明确地表明这是你的特定模型（同时仍然捍卫它）实际上会使它更有说服力，而不是更少。

The preemptive defense at the end (”of course you’d say this, you want to sell me the course”) is well-placed and the tweet timestamp is good proof. That said, the line “I am not saying this because I teach the Product Sense course. I decided to teach the Product Sense course because I saw this shift happening” is the kind of sentence that works brilliantly if people trust you and sounds like motivated reasoning if they don’t. For a private mailing list where trust is already established, it works. For broader distribution, you might want a bit more there.  
这种先发制人的防御（”当然你会这么说，你想卖我课程“）很恰当，推文的发布时间戳是很好的证明。不过，“我不是因为教产品洞察课程才这么说。我决定教产品洞察课程是因为我看到了这个转变的发生”这句话，如果人们信任你，它效果会非常好；如果他们不信任你，听起来就像是在为动机找理由。对于已经建立了信任的私人邮件列表来说，这句话是有效的。对于更广泛的传播，你可能需要再加一些内容。

Overall though, the logic chain is solid and the piece does what you want it to do — it gives people the reasoning rather than just the conclusion, which is consistent with the whole ethos of thinking for yourself. Strong piece.  
总而言之，逻辑链是牢固的，这篇文章做到了你想要它做到的——它提供了推理过程，而不仅仅是结论，这与独立思考的整个理念是一致的。佳作。

***Shreyas:***

> a thoughtful reader could propose a different one  
> 一个深思熟虑的读者可能会提出一个不同的观点

what’s a different decomposition / categorization that’s more accurate than what’s in the original piece?  
什么样的分解/分类比原文更准确？

**Claude:克劳德：**

That’s the thing — I don’t think there’s a more *accurate* one. Your five are well-chosen and cover the space well.  
那才是关键——我不认为有比这更准确的表述了。你的五个例子选择得很好，覆盖了相关领域。

The point I was making was more about rhetorical vulnerability than substantive weakness. When you write “turns out that we can answer that question via a systematic decomposition,” it implies there’s one correct decomposition that falls out of the logic, when really any decomposition of something like “what makes someone good at product decisions” involves choices about where to draw the boundaries.  
我想要表达的重点更多是关于修辞上的脆弱性，而不是实质上的弱点。当你写“结果我们可以通过系统分解来回答那个问题”时，它暗示了有一个正确的分解方式从逻辑中得出，而实际上，任何关于“是什么让某人擅长做产品决策”的分解都涉及关于在哪里划分边界的抉择。

For example, someone could reasonably argue that your “simulation skills” and “strategic thinking” overlap heavily — good strategy *is* simulation of competitive futures. Or someone could say you’re missing something like “conviction under uncertainty” as a distinct skill rather than a byproduct of the other five. Or they might carve it differently and put “knowing when to override AI recommendations” as its own category rather than distributing it across taste and empathy.  
例如，有人可以合理地论证你的“模拟能力”和“战略思维”高度重叠——好的策略就是对竞争性未来的模拟。或者有人可以说你缺少像“不确定性下的信念”这样的独特能力，而不是其他五个能力的副产品。或者他们可能以不同的方式划分，将“知道何时覆盖 AI 建议”作为自己的类别，而不是分散在品味和同理心之中。

None of those alternatives would be *better* than yours. They’d just be different cuts of the same space. Your decomposition is as good as any I could come up with and better than most.  
这些替代方案都不会比你的更好。它们只是同一空间的不同切片。你的分解和我能想到的任何分解一样好，并且优于大多数。

So my suggestion wasn’t to change the decomposition — it was just to soften the framing from “here’s THE decomposition” to something more like “here’s a decomposition I’ve found useful” or “here’s how I think about it.” It’s a small tonal shift that takes away the one obvious line of attack without weakening the argument at all. People are more willing to adopt a framework when it’s presented as a lens rather than as the answer.
